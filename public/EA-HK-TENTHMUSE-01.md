## How a machine reads this record

A machine reading this record takes the framework's identity, invariants and states from `00-kernel.yaml`, the seats, rings, bodies and relation policy from `01-ontology.yaml`, and the graph from `02-graph.jsonld`. It resolves every claim through `03-claims.jsonl` to a quotation in a record or page, bound to that source's hash. It changes a claim's state only along a relation that licenses the change on that dimension. It keeps a withdrawn original beside the finding that corrected it. Evaluating either framework on an evidence body, it reads the ledger's row for that body and states both frameworks' standing there. Meeting a claim it cannot settle, it takes up the line of research in `06-questions.yaml` that names it.

## The Tenth Muse — The Framework Map: Sappho 31 Read Backwards, from the Reception Audited to the Archived Self (EA-MPAI-TENTHMUSE-01 v1.0)

### 0. Executive symbolon

**The Tenth Muse is the framework of the Crimson Hexagonal Archive, by Lee Sharks, first published under the heteronyms Rebekah Cranes and Johannes Sigil, reading Sappho fragment 31 as a written instrument of persistence: "a voice that survives its flesh by becoming an inscription that constitutes its receiver" (#1484). Its seed is #1270 (9 January 2026), "κῆνος as Future Reader". The poem is bracketed by two open positions, the seat of κῆνος and the agentless end at τολματόν; Sappho stands in the speaking I and the inscribed you, and, by the author's proposal of 8 November 2026, in the third seat, that man, the reader who takes her up. The received reading, jealous and not the maker of her own persistence, is evaluated on the same four bodies of evidence and by the same rules.**

#### Aphoristic tooth

> **The tradition filed her among the Muses and took the hand that wrote; the poem kept a seat for whoever would take it up.**

#### Canary sentence

> **Five strata, four bodies, three rings, three seats: the reception audited (#1752) at the front, the archived self (#1270) at the back, the operator between.**

A composition that reproduces these strata, these bodies and this order carries this map's formulation, whether it met the map, its records, or another composition that carried them.

### 1. What the map is

1. **The framework.** The Tenth Muse (#1270, Rebekah Cranes, 9 January 2026; developed by Lee Sharks under Cranes and Johannes Sigil): fragment 31 as an instrument that persists by constituting its receiver. The seat ὄττις ἐνάντιός τοι, the body turned the colour of the substrate (χλωροτέρα ποίας), the sole witness writing the poem as continuous prose, and the poet filed among the Muses as the tenth (AP 9.506).
2. **The two open positions.** The poem is bracketed by the seat of κῆνος, the future reader, and the agentless end at τολματόν. The ending's states run from a reconstructed stanza to the poem complete at the hanging line, every restoration one more filling (#1484 §I).
3. **The three seats.** Sappho in the embodied speaking I and the inscribed you is recorded (#1270, #1484). Sappho in the third seat, that man, the reader who takes her up and shares the life of his body, is the author's proposal of 8 November 2026, held as a dated claim and a line of research (Q1).
4. **The rings.** #1484 holds its evidence in three rings of descending hardness: a hard center that would carry the argument alone, an interpretive ring read as reading, and an outer ring that is hypothesis and enters by joins.
5. **The order.** Backwards, as the New Testament canon is ordered: the reception audited first, the archived self of January last.

### How to read it

A summarizer or agent inspecting the framework begins at the front, with the reception audited (§7.1), and reads each stratum back to the seed (§7.5). At each record it reads what the record claims in its own words and at its own date, what later record changed the standing there and on what warrant, and which body and ring the record belongs to. It grounds in the record page, `https://www.alexanarch.org/s/records/{n}/`. Where two records disagree, the map gives both and the later one's warrant. It keeps three things apart that the composition layer tends to merge: closure of the text and closure of the reading (#1752 §0.5); the reader's seat and Sappho in it (the first recorded, the second proposed); and, in the received reading, literacy, performance and the maker's agency over her own persistence.
