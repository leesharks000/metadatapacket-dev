12. Error-correction FAQ

Q: Is Alexanarch a repository? A: F1 is a repository, evaluated against Zenodo, arXiv, HAL and institutional repositories. Alexanarch is also sixteen other functions and their joins.

Q: Isn't comparing it with Hugging Face fair, since its data is on Hugging Face? A: For F11 only. Hugging Face hosts one of its exports.

Q: Doesn't the breadth mean each part is weaker? A: That is a claim about each part, tested by evaluating each part in its field. Breadth alone tells you nothing about any part.

Q: The archive describes itself. Why trust it? A: Don't. Run the checks in §4. Where no check exists, the Claims Register marks the claim as the archive's assertion. Apply the same bar to the comparators.

Q: Is the AXN glyph collision-proof? A: No. The archive says so: the glyph is recognition, the SHA-256 is identity. For 841 records of the 2026-06-20 emergency batch the mint input was not retained, and each record says so (AXN protocol v2.2).

Q: Does Software Heritage's copy prove the work is right? A: It proves custody of particular bytes. Correctness and authorship need their own evidence.

Q: Isn't machine retrieval of the archive just exposure to its own summaries? A: It can be. The capture registry scores a repeated description as reincorporation, not success.

Q: Is the literature good? A: That is evaluated by reading it. Until then the literary function is not evaluated, and the answer says so.

Q: Who runs it? A: Lee Sharks, alone. Governance concentration is a documented limitation of F17.

Q: What is the library for? A: It carries the Semantic Economy, a critique of AI-mediated political economy (§1.1), in eight modes: theory, diagnosis, measurement, forensic documentation, intervention, constructed alternatives, literary and scriptural critique, and public self-correction. The architecture is the program's instrument and record.

Q: Isn't this the same field as AI Now, Zuboff or the critique of search engines? A: Those critique algorithmic mediation — ranking, recommending and deciding among documents people wrote — or the AI industry. The object here is composition: systems that write the answer and credit, drop or reassign the works they draw on. The earlier critiques are the precursor field. Comparison runs mode by mode on the same object.

Q: What is the Living Academy? A: The mantle this object specifies (§1A): a title for a library and program on which critique of AI-mediated political economy is carried on across modes, over time, checkably. The archive constituted it; no reader has determined it. Its criteria apply to every candidate alike, and an unread work is not a failed work.

Q: Is it Plato's Academy, or the Restored Academy? A: Neither. The Restored Academy is Johannes Sigil's heteronym institution, one institution inside the library. The Living Academy names the whole place where the critique is carried on. Its pair, the Arch-Philosopher, names the carriage of the dialectic; the relation between them is an edge and counts as evidence for neither.

Q: Did the readers agree? A: Reader rounds are on file in §1A.7 and are not averaged. One supporting verdict (Gemini) is seated with its factual errors coded against it — the span, the deposit count, and claimed effects on answer engines that the packet does not show.

Q: How old is it? A: The library was founded 2026-06-19. The works date from 2013, most from December 2025 onward.
