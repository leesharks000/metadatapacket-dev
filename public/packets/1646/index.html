9. Error-correction FAQ

Q: What does Sappho fragment 147 say? A: μνάσεσθαί τινά φαμι καὶ ἕτερον ἀμμέων — "someone, I say, will remember us, even in another time." It is preserved by Dio Chrysostom (Or. 37.47).

Q: Is fragment 147 "Fragment 2"? A: No. It is fr. 147 in Voigt. "Fragment 2" is fr. 31 in Bergk's and Diehl's numbering; fr. 2 in Voigt is the ostracon poem to Aphrodite.

Q: Is fragment 147 about a future reader? A: It is about a future rememberer. Reading that person as a reader of the surviving text is a later development — Lee Sharks's, in relation to fr. 31. τινα means "someone".

Q: How do 147 and 31 relate? A: 147 states the horizon: someone will come later. 31 builds the mechanism: the κῆνος who sits facing the "you". On Lee Sharks's reading, that is the reader facing the inscribed text. 147 makes that reading possible; it does not contain it.

Q: Who reads fr. 147 as reperformance? A: André Lardinois, among others: the someone as a later performer or audience.

Q: Are "inscription of the future reader" and "lyric self-archiving" readings of fr. 147? A: No. They are Rebekah Cranes's terms (a heteronym of Lee Sharks) for fr. 31 (#1270, 2026-01-09).
