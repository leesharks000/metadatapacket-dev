**Standard discourse.** The Apocalypse is dated c. 95 under Domitian and read as the last book of the New Testament and a receiver of earlier tradition. On the two-source hypothesis Mark is the earliest synoptic gospel and Matthew and Luke each used it; the double tradition, the some two hundred verses Matthew and Luke share and Mark lacks, mostly sayings, often in close or identical Greek, is assigned to a common written source, Q, from the German *Quelle*, source. No manuscript, citation or fragment of Q is known (Streeter 1924, p. 184: "Q, if such a document ever existed, has disappeared"). Farrer's account (1955) dispenses with Q by having Luke use Matthew.

**The proposed extension.** Revelation First holds that the writings of the New Testament unfold from the Apocalypse as generative source (#202, #1735), and that the cosmic Christ of Rev 1 is the originary image the epistles extract and the gospels unfold (#407, #636). #1735 keeps three claims apart: absolute priority, corpus priority and generative priority. *Revelation = Q* is the claim that the extant Apocalypse satisfies many of the properties the field requires of Q (§2.12), and that at the points where the field's own measures can be run, the seven, the triad, the crown, the mourning tribes and two sayings of the double tradition, the Apocalypse shares with a later writing a mark found nowhere else, and each arrow is stated at its grade. Evidentiary status in a source argument is read through four powers: **presupposition** (entering as a premise without prior proof), **assignment** (receiving material from other texts), **text** (being given a wording, an apparatus, an edition) and **candidacy** (being admitted wherever a source is sought) (#1746 §1.3).

**The relation between them.** The packet decides no solution to the synoptic problem, and a demonstration that Q existed leaves its claim standing (#1746 §8.2). It claims no derivation of the double tradition as a whole from the Apocalypse. It reads an extant text against the specification of an imaginary one, and reports where the specification is met.

## 2. Review of the evidence

### 2.1 The date

The archive holds 66 CE as the working proposed date of composition (#1737 §8, 2026-11-04), held as proposed until its case is formally argued. Its grounds, as deposited: the temple stands in the book (Rev 11:1, "μέτρησον τὸν ναὸν τοῦ Θεοῦ καὶ τὸ θυσιαστήριον"); the number of the beast is Nero in Hebrew gematria (666, 616); the fall of Jerusalem is absent (#202, Claim 1); the book addresses seven living churches (1:11); and the portents Josephus dates to the spring of 66 (BJ 6.288–315: the light at the altar, the gate opened of itself, the armies in the air, the voice in the sanctuary at Pentecost) stand beside the book's in thirteen rows (#1737). The archive's earlier ranges stand as recorded: pre-70 (#636, #202) and 68–73 (#407, #10). The critics read in #1746 who dated the book place it before Matthew as well: Colani at 68 (1864, p. 209), Keim at Easter 69 (vol. III, 1872).

The date of c. 95 is the reception's. Its external anchor is one sentence of Irenaeus (*Adv. Haer.* 5.30.3), ἑωράθη, its subject unexpressed; Charles (1920) explained the evidence for earlier dates as survivals in sources and filled the silence by presumption (#1741 §4.2, K9). Where the conventional date appears unmarked in the archive's own voice, it is recorded as an erratum (#1737 §8). On the held date the book stands before Matthew and Luke on the common dating of both after 70, and before or beside Mark, which the field places in the late 60s or early 70s. Against the letters of Paul the received chronology sets 1 Thessalonians about 50–51, and the date has one material anchor. The Delphi inscription dates Gallio's proconsulship of Achaia (on the usual restoration, 51–52); it names neither Paul nor Thessalonica. Acts 18:12–17 places Paul before Gallio, and an itinerary built from Acts 17–18 and 1 Thess 3:1–6 joins the letter to that visit. The stone dates a proconsul; the corpus's own narrative carries the date to Paul, and the corpus's own order carries it to the letter (#1735 §II.2: "anchored at exactly one end"). **The circularity is the one #1746 reads in Q: a date for the corpus derived from the corpus.** The particular dependence is this. The question under examination is the order in which the writings of the New Testament were composed. The date set against the Apocalypse reaches the letter through Acts, a writing of the same collection, whose narrative of Paul is the received order. To let that date decide against the Apocalypse is to let the collection's account of its own order decide a question about that order (#1735 §II.2: "a corpus’s self-narrated chronology cannot foreclose a question about that corpus’s own composition history"). #202 holds the Pauline chronology inferential (Claim 2). The packet treats the received date of 1 Thessalonians as that chain, and gives it no standing as an independent date.

### 2.2 The ancestry is extant

#1735 §I sets four classes of ancestry for any load-bearing feature: an extant antecedent, an independently attested tradition, a reconstructed antecedent "inferred primarily because the later text seems to require it", and an unexplained innovation. The received sequence begins with the literature whose immediate Christian ancestry must be reconstructed, as the field's category "pre-Pauline" concedes, and ends with the one book whose ancestry can be read: the Apocalypse's machinery has extant antecedents in the Septuagint (Daniel, Ezekiel, Zechariah, Isaiah, Exodus), and the book cites none of them by formula (#1741 K7: γέγραπται only of names in the book of life, 13:8, 17:8; "ἐτελέσθη τὸ μυστήριον τοῦ Θεοῦ, ὡς εὐηγγέλισεν τοὺς ἑαυτοῦ δούλους τοὺς προφήτας", 10:7). Q is the field's founding instance of the third class (#1746 §1.4).

### 2.3 Toward the epistles

- **The seven-church corpus** (#1735 §IV). Rev 1–3 is the earliest extant Christian text constructed as a closed address to seven named churches: "Ὃ βλέπεις γράψον εἰς βιβλίον καὶ πέμψον ταῖς ἑπτὰ ἐκκλησίαις" (1:11). No surviving evidence places a sevenfold Pauline collection before it. The Muratorian Fragment says Paul wrote to seven churches *sequens prodecessoris sui Iohannis ordine*, following the order of his predecessor John. Frankfurter (2024), a Pauline-priority account, concedes the absence of an early sevenfold collection and calls Revelation's seven archetypal. Charles (1920) answered the fragment on the dates of the component letters, moving its claim from the architecture to the persons' dates (#1741 K3). *Grade:* structural archetype established; generation the economical reading.
- **The triad** (#1735 §V). Rev 2:2, "Οἶδα τὰ ἔργα σου καὶ τὸν κόπον καὶ τὴν ὑπομονήν σου"; 1 Thess 1:3, "τοῦ ἔργου τῆς πίστεως καὶ τοῦ κόπου τῆς ἀγάπης καὶ τῆς ὑπομονῆς τῆς ἐλπίδος". Across some 69,400 units of the Septuagint, the New Testament, the Apostolic Fathers, Philo, Josephus and the Greek pseudepigrapha, the ordered triple occurs in two units, and κόπος with ὑπομονή is unique to the same two verses. **The Apocalypse has the bare sequence; Paul has it expanded by the three theological genitives.** *Grade:* markedness established. #1735 leaves the direction of the genitive expansion undetermined and records the arrow withheld by the received date of 1 Thessalonians, the chain of §2.1, which the packet does not grant as an independent date.
- **The crown of life** (#1735 §VII). Rev 2:10, "γίνου πιστὸς ἄχρι θανάτου, καὶ δώσω σοι τὸν στέφανον τῆς ζωῆς", a promise performed in the first person; Jas 1:12, "λήμψεται τὸν στέφανον τῆς ζωῆς, ὃν ἐπηγγείλατο τοῖς ἀγαπῶσιν αὐτόν", the promise presupposed. In some seventy thousand units the formula is unique to the two verses. The common-source audit was run against its best candidates, 4 Maccabees and the Testament of Job (4.4–10), which supply the field and not the formula. The reception trace stands at the addressed church: the Martyrdom of Polycarp awards the bishop of Smyrna the crown (17.1; 19.2). Jas 1:12 is the epistle's thesis statement on Eng's structural analysis (2022; the directional claim is #1735's own). Charles gave the crown to the games, James a "cf." (#1741 K6). *Grade:* a serious local result, the arrow drawn.
- **The forms** (#202, Claim 4, held): the seven letters as the epistolary form; the Lamb of Rev 5 as the matter of Pauline atonement; the New Jerusalem as the eschatology; the throne-room as the architecture of worship.

### 2.4 Toward the gospels

- **The titles** (#1741 K1). Three books title themselves by Jesus Christ: "Βίβλος γενέσεως Ἰησοῦ Χριστοῦ" (Matt 1:1), "Ἀρχὴ τοῦ εὐαγγελίου Ἰησοῦ Χριστοῦ" (Mark 1:1), "Ἀποκάλυψις Ἰησοῦ Χριστοῦ" (Rev 1:1).
- **The fourfold form** (#1741 K5). Irenaeus founds the fourfold gospel on the four living creatures of Rev 4:7, quoting the book; the book lends the figure and is not counted among the four.
- **The mourning tribes and the clouds** (#1746 §III–IV). Hitzig (1843) argued from Matt 24:30 and Rev 1:7 that Matthew used the Apocalypse, "einen zwingenden Beweis", on the order of seeing and mourning, the Zechariah frame and Matthew's missing ἐπ’ αὐτόν. A frozen census of some 2.53 million words of seated Greek finds the mourning of all the tribes of the earth joined to the coming with the clouds in those two verses and nowhere else; the elements have extant sources in Dan 7:13, Zech 12:10–12 and Gen 12:3, and the census decides no direction. The six critics are set out in §2.8.
- **Two sayings of the double tradition** (observation). *The thief* (Q 12:39): Matt 24:43 has ποίᾳ φυλακῇ and ἐγρηγόρησεν; Luke 12:39 has ποίᾳ ὥρᾳ and no watching; Rev 3:3, "ἐὰν οὖν μὴ γρηγορήσῃς, ἥξω ὡς κλέπτης, καὶ οὐ μὴ γνῷς ποίαν ὥραν ἥξω ἐπὶ σέ", has the watching of Matthew and the hour of Luke in one saying, and Rev 16:15, "Ἰδοὺ ἔρχομαι ὡς κλέπτης· μακάριος ὁ γρηγορῶν". *The confession* (Q 12:8): Matt 10:32, "ὁμολογήσω κἀγὼ ἐν αὐτῷ ἔμπροσθεν τοῦ Πατρός μου τοῦ ἐν τοῖς οὐρανοῖς"; Luke 12:8, "ὁμολογήσει ἐν αὐτῷ ἔμπροσθεν τῶν ἀγγέλων τοῦ Θεοῦ"; Rev 3:5, "ὁμολογήσω τὸ ὄνομα αὐτοῦ ἐνώπιον τοῦ Πατρός μου καὶ ἐνώπιον τῶν ἀγγέλων αὐτοῦ", the Father of Matthew and the angels of Luke in one saying, in the first person as in Matthew. **In both, the Apocalypse holds together what the two gospels divide, the work the field assigns to Q's wording.** The parallels are known (L. A. Vos, *The Synoptic Traditions in the Apocalypse*, 1965; in copyright, declared); the observation of the division is made here on two sayings, by reading, and is to be tested by a frozen census across the double tradition. Hitzig named the thief beside Matt 24:30 in 1843. The observation states the problem and chooses no history. Three accounts fit it: the Apocalypse supplies a formulation the gospels later divide; the Apocalypse combines formulations already divided; or the three texts preserve and reshape an antecedent formulation differently. The next test weighs them by contextual coherence, distinctive wording, sequence, the direction of each transformation and the textual variants, beside the distribution, and it includes the double-tradition sayings that resist the reading, so the two sayings read here do not become the specification.

### 2.5 The figure

The cosmic Christ of Rev 1:12–16 is the originary compressed image, which the epistles extract and the gospels unfold (#407); the Apocalypse is the originary scripture from which the New Testament was decompressed (#636); the quest for the historical Jesus is redirected toward the historical Logos (#902). The book names its figure four times in 19:11–16, two names written and two called, one withheld: "κέκληται τὸ ὄνομα αὐτοῦ Ὁ Λόγος τοῦ Θεοῦ" (19:13); Irenaeus quotes the passage whole, the Word *non in una figura*, and assigns no direction of dependence between it and the Gospel's Logos (#1741 K4). On the archive's reading, if the book comes first, the Logos it depicts is a work of literature before it is a historical person (#1741 §0.1). *Grade:* held position.

The record is of local judgments never incorporated into the models, including the models of the critics who made them. ## 3. Three levels of difference

- **Usage level.** "Q" in the field names a hypothetical document. In *Revelation = Q* the letter is read as a specification, the properties a source must have to do the work the field gives Q, at its etymology, *Quelle*, source. The field's Q is posited to meet the specification; the packet reads an extant text against it.
- **Method level.** The field tests Q by the relations among extant texts it is invoked to explain. The programme tests the Apocalypse by frozen censuses of markedness across the seated Greek, by audits of common source against the best candidates, and by reading at the page which candidates each source theory admitted.
- **Radical level.** The field asks which lost document best explains the gospels. Revelation First asks whether the New Testament's load-bearing forms have an extant antecedent among its own books, and holds that they do, in the book the collection placed last.

## 12. Error-correction FAQ

**When was Revelation written, on the archive's position?** The archive holds 66 CE, proposed until its case is formally argued (#1737 §8), on the temple standing in 11:1–2, the number as Nero, the absence of the fall, and the portents of spring 66. The c. 95 date rests on one sentence of Irenaeus whose subject is unexpressed.

**Is Revelation First the claim that Revelation was written first?** It separates absolute, corpus and generative priority (#1735), and its centre is generative: the writings that follow unfold from the Apocalypse (#202). The early-date view is another claim.

**Does any epistle depend on Revelation?** #1735 finds the crown of life unique to Rev 2:10 and Jas 1:12 in some seventy thousand units, the promise performed in one and presupposed in the other, with the common-source audit run: a serious local result. It finds the ordered triad of works, toil and endurance unique to Rev 2:2 and 1 Thess 1:3, with the arrow withheld; the received date of 1 Thessalonians set against it rests on an inscription that dates Gallio and on Acts, which places Paul before him.

**Did anyone before the archive argue that Matthew used Revelation?** Yes: Hitzig in 1843, "einen zwingenden Beweis"; Volkmar; Keim, in a date; Bernhard Weiss, "nach Apoc. 1, 7 eingefügt"; Bousset; Merx, a gloss "wörtlich aus Apocalypse 1, 7 entlehnt".

**Has the dependence been refuted?** No work read answers Hitzig's argument from the text. Keim and Allen set against it a common usage of which no copy is cited.

**Does the packet say Q did not exist?** It decides no solution to the synoptic problem. It reads the standing Q was given beside the book that exists.

**What would count against Revelation = Q?** An attested source of a shared mark earlier than the Apocalypse and available to the later writer; an argument from the text that meets Hitzig's, or shows a mark's form in the Apocalypse presupposing the later writing's; a census rerun on the public plans that finds a mark elsewhere; double-tradition material that resists the properties reading; a chronology for the Apocalypse that excludes the direction, argued from evidence outside the corpus (#1735 §VIII; #1746 §VIII).

**How many works does #1746 read?** Eighteen, from Lachmann (1835) to Streeter (1924): eight in seat (the seven of EA-CORPORA-35, and Bousset in EA-CORPORA-34) and ten by reference in hashed text layers (Lachmann, Wilke, Hitzig, Colani, Weizsäcker, Weiffenbach, Keim, B. Weiss, Merx, Allen).
